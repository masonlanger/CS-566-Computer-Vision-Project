# Implementation
## WorldTrack

### SSM

First, we define a per-object *state-space model* (SSM) in which each image-space detection $o_t=[u_t, v_t]$ is explained by an underlying world-space latent state $s_t=[x_t, y_t, \dot x_t, \dot y_t]$. The latent state evolves according to 

$$
s_{t+1}=f_\theta(s_t)+w,\quad w\sim\mathcal N(0,Q)
$$

where $f_\theta$ is the transition model, which may be learned or specified heuristically. In our implementation, we use a heuristic transition model of the form $$f_\theta(s_t)=\begin{bmatrix}x_t+\dot x_t\Delta t\\y_t+\dot y_t\Delta t\\\alpha\dot x_t\\\alpha\dot y_t\end{bmatrix}.$$

where $\alpha<1$ is a hyperparameter used to decay the velocities over time. Each world-space position is projected onto the image plane using a world-to-image homography $H_t$ which we assume is obtained from an external camera-calibration pipeline.

$$
o_{t}=H_t\begin{bmatrix}x_t\\y_t\\1\end{bmatrix}+w,\quad w\sim\mathcal N(0,R)
$$

Finally, we assume the initial state $s_0$ is generated from some distribution $\mathcal N(\mu_0,\Sigma_0)$. We discuss how this distribution is obtained below.

### Bayesian State Estimation

Given the SSM formulation above, we use Bayesian state estimators to compute posteriors over latent states conditioned on sequences of observations. These methods are broadly classified into *filtering* algorithms which compute the state distribution at each frame using only past observations and *smoothing* algorithms which refine these estimates by incorporating future observations.

In our implementation, we use a bootstrap particle filter (BPF) and a backward simulation particle smoother (BSPS). Particle-based estimators are well suited for non-linear SSMs but are computationally expensive. While our current heuristic transition model is linear and could, in principle, be handled by a standard Kalman estimator, we use particle-based methods to ensure that the overall algorithm readily extends to more expressive (e.g., a neural network) transition models.

### Data Association

The following section discusses the data association process which is needed to match image detections to state posteriors. Data association occurs after the prediction step and before the update step of the BPF. Once data association is complete, unassociated posteriors carry forward the predicted particle set while associated posteriors are updated with respect to their associated detections. 

For each existing track at frame $t$, we compute the likelihood that a given detection was generated by that track by integrating the observation likelihood over the set of particles obtained from the BPF prediction step. Concretely, for track $k$ with particles $\{s^{(m)}_{t}\}_{m=1}^M$ and candidate detection $o_t^{(i)}$, the association likelihood is given by,
$$
\ell_{k,i}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m}\mathcal{N}\left(o_t^{(i)};\ H_t\begin{bmatrix}x^{(m)}_{t}\\y^{(m)}_{t}\\1\end{bmatrix},R\right)
$$

Since posterior distributions in this context are typically unimodal and near-Gaussian, it may be sufficient to use the posterior mean.

The cost matrix used for assignment is then constructed as $C_{k,i}=-\log\ell_{k,i}$ where a chi-squared threshold is applied to reject implausible matches. We then use the Hungarian algorithm to compute the optimal assignment of detections to tracks.

### Track Birth & Death

In general, the number of active tracks and detections in a given frame will differ, leading to both unassigned tracks and unassigned detections. These cases naturally give rise to track birth (from unassigned detections) and track death (from repeatedly unassigned tracks).

**Track birth**. An unassigned detection $[x_t^{(i)}, y_t^{(i)}]$ represents a potential new object entering the scene and is thus used to initialize a new per-object SSM with initial state distribution $\mathcal N(\mu_0, \Sigma_0)$. The mean $\mu_0$ is computed by projecting the detection into world coordinates; since the velocities are unknown at birth, we initialize them to zero. 

$$
\mu_0=\begin{bmatrix}x_t \\y_t\\0 \\0\end{bmatrix},\quad \begin{bmatrix}
x_t\\y_t\\1\end{bmatrix}= H_t^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}u_t\\v_t\\1\end{bmatrix}
$$

The position component of the initial state covariance is obtained by propagating the observation noise covariance $R$ into world-space; this can be done by computing $J R J^\top$ where $J$ is the Jacobian of the mapping induced by $H^{-1}_t$ at $\mu_0$. The velocity components are heuristically defined.

**Track death**. A track that is consecutively unassigned for $U=50$ frames is automatically terminated. In addition, we terminate any unassigned track whose posterior drifts into an implausible region of the world (in this case, the playable area of the soccer field).

## Results

We evaluate WorldTrack on the [SoccerNet Tracking dataset](https://www.soccer-net.org/tasks/tracking); since our method does not address detection or homography estimation, we use the ground-truth detections provided by SoccerNet and obtain homographies using the [TVCalib](https://mm4spa.github.io/tvcalib/) algorithm.

We also compare our method to ByteTrack, a widely used image-space tracking algorithm. ByteTrack is a particularly informative baseline because, like WorldTrack, it relies solely on geometric information for association and does not factor in appearance—this allows us to attribute any performance gains to the fundamental advantage of world-space tracking over image-space tracking in this domain. To ensure a fair comparison, we provide ByteTrack with the same ground-truth detections used in our method.

We evaluate both methods using the *association accuracy* (AssA) metric which measures a tracker’s ability to maintain consistent identities over time. Higher AssA indicates fewer identity switches and more stable long-term associations. The table below summarizes the AssA scores for WorldTrack and ByteTrack across 20 videos (each 200 frames).

| AssA | Mean | STD |
| - | - | - |
| ByteTrack | 0.568 | 0.215 |
| WorldTrack | 0.830 | 0.052 |

WorldTrack achieves a higher mean AssA and lower standard deviation. The reduced variance indicates that WorldTrack remains stable across diverse camera motions, whereas ByteTrack exhibits larger fluctuations due to its reliance on image-space stability.
